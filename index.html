01 — The Purpose

The compute divide is
already inside the university.

Industry-level projects in the AI era require GPUs because they process thousands of tensor operations in parallel — exactly what modern AI models, simulation, rendering, and computer vision demand. Meanwhile, the gap between what students are taught and what students can actually run has become structural.

What a student actually hits

A laptop RTX with 8 GB of VRAM runs a 7B language model, slowly. It cannot fine-tune one. It cannot generate a 720p video clip. It cannot hold a robotics simulation with visual sensors and a policy network at the same time. It cannot serve a model to a room of twenty workshop attendees.

The student does not lack the skill. The student lacks memory bandwidth and VRAM — and those are purchasable.

03 — The Proposal

Install a GPU suite in the Smart Lab.
Operate it like infrastructure.

Not a club purchase. A transparent, logged, managed resource for approved student projects and workshops — with a real governance model attached before a single card is ordered.

Host

Smart Lab

We have already spoken with Professor Jeremy Hajek, Smart Lab Director, and the Smart Lab is comfortable with us installing a GPU cluster/suite. The hosting pathway exists — this proposal is about hardware, not about finding a room.

Smart Tech Lab ↗ Prof. Hajek ↗
Access

VPN → SSH

Students connect through approved Illinois Tech network controls. Access is individual, logged, and revocable — every job is attributable to a named user who signed a usage policy. No shared credentials, no anonymous compute.

Administration

Student admins

Student technical administrators handle setup, documentation, maintenance, and onboarding under Smart Lab oversight — with a defined succession path so the system outlives any single graduating class.

This gives Illinois Tech a student-facing computing solution — not another club purchase for one organization to use exclusively.
04 — The Architecture

Students bring ideas and skill.
The system handles access, compute, and accountability.

Five stages, one accountability layer. Designed so that "who ran what, when, and for how long" is always answerable.

STAGE 02

STAGE 03

The Prospect

Hugging Face is the curriculum. GPUs are the classroom.

Hugging Face hosts millions of open models, datasets, and applications — free to download, free to run, free to modify. It is the single largest body of production AI knowledge available to a student, and almost all of it is inaccessible without local VRAM.

A GPU suite converts an open ecosystem the university already has free access to into hands-on, résumé-grade experience.

Excludes staff time, which the student administration model contributes at no cost to the university — that is the point of the governance structure.

Owning enables what renting cannot

No per-minute anxiety. Students experiment, fail, and retry — which is how learning works and exactly what metered billing punishes.

Data stays on campus

Research data, student work, and any FERPA-adjacent material never leave Illinois Tech infrastructure. That is a compliance advantage, not just a cost one.

The asset persists

Cloud credits expire and leave nothing behind. A GPU is a depreciating asset that still serves students in year five — and can be donated forward.

09 — The Venture Case

Compute is where
student companies start.

Every AI company begins as somebody's unfunded experiment. The difference between a project and a startup is usually whether the founder could run the thing long enough to show it to someone. That is a hardware question before it is a business question.

The Chicago precedent

In April 2026, the University of Chicago's Polsky Center announced a partnership with the AI Research Commons, Microsoft, and NVIDIA to accelerate Midwest AI startups — bringing inception-stage companies up to $350K in cloud credits, NVIDIA Inception benefits, and Bay Area investor introductions.

Seven miles north of our campus, a peer institution built the on-ramp. Illinois Tech has the engineering culture, the Smart Lab, and the student organizations already in place. What it does not have is the compute.

Read the announcement ↗

For business and entrepreneurship majors

The Compute Collaborative is not only an engineering project. It is a small operating business with a capital budget, a utilization model, a service-level obligation, a governance charter, and a succession plan.

  • ▸ Capital allocation — justify a five-figure purchase with a utilization forecast.
  • ▸ Unit economics — measure and report true cost per GPU-hour delivered.
  • ▸ Vendor negotiation — education pricing, warranty terms, procurement cycles.
  • ▸ Sponsor relations — manage a corporate partnership and report against it.
  • ▸ Stakeholder governance — allocate a scarce resource across five organizations, fairly and visibly.

Kellogg-style case studies are taught from decks. This one would be lived from a budget line.

Specifically for NVIDIA

The programs already exist. We fit them.

NVIDIA's Academic Hardware Grant Program has historically donated workstation-class GPUs — RTX 6000 Ada, RTX A6000, and datacenter cards for higher-impact projects — at valuations of roughly $5,000 to $25,000. The main track is not accepting new applications as of August 2026, but the AI Safety track runs on a rolling deadline and remains open globally.

Alongside hardware, the DLI Teaching Kit and University Ambassador programs give Illinois Tech certified instructors, free student course access worth up to $90 per course per student, and curriculum that maps directly onto the workloads this suite would run.

A GPU in the Smart Lab is not charity. It is a certified-instructor pipeline, a Chicago-region recruiting channel, and a demonstrable answer to the academic compute divide that NVIDIA's own leadership describes as an economic problem.
